7 Signs Trauma Is Still
Affecting You After an Accident

Trauma does not always announce itself. Here are seven signs it may still be running in the background.

Trauma does not always look like what you would expect. Sometimes it shows up as feeling emotionally flat, being easily startled, or simply not feeling like yourself anymore. If you were in a car accident, ICBC counselling in Nanaimo can help you make sense of what you are noticing.

1. Constantly Waiting for Something Bad to Happen

Hypervigilance — your nervous system always scanning for threat. It is exhausting and often invisible to others. This is one of the clearest signs anxiety counselling in Nanaimo could help.

2. Small Things Set You Off

Irritability and emotional reactivity are classic trauma symptoms. Snapping at people, losing patience faster than usual — often a sign your nervous system is in overdrive. This shows up often in men's counselling in Nanaimo, since irritability is frequently the more socially acceptable emotion to show.

3. You Are Sleeping Differently

Difficulty falling asleep, waking in the night, nightmares, or sleeping too much — all can be trauma responses. Your nervous system is struggling to settle.

4. You Feel Disconnected

Emotional numbness, feeling detached, going through the motions — this is dissociation. The nervous system managing overwhelming experience. Trauma counselling in Nanaimo is built specifically for this.

5. You Are Avoiding Things

Avoiding driving, avoiding the intersection, avoiding conversations about the accident. Avoidance is one of the clearest signs something has not been fully processed — and it can quietly strain a relationship over time, which is where couples counselling in Nanaimo can help.

6. Your Body Is Holding It

Unexplained tension, chronic headaches, a tight chest — the body stores what the mind has not fully processed. See why anxiety feels physical.

7. You Feel Like You Should Be Over It By Now

One of the most common things people say in a first session. Trauma does not follow a timeline. Feeling like you should be further along is often a sign that support would help.

Can these symptoms show up months after an accident?
Yes — delayed trauma responses are common. The nervous system sometimes suppresses symptoms until the immediate crisis has passed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can trauma symptoms show up months or years later?

Yes. Trauma doesn't follow a strict timeline, it's common for symptoms to surface well after the event itself.

What's the difference between stress and unresolved trauma?

Stress tends to ease once the situation passes. Unresolved trauma keeps your nervous system on alert even when you're safe.

Do I need a diagnosis to start counselling?

No. You don't need a formal diagnosis to get support, noticing that something feels off is enough.
