Life After an Accident
A Guide to Getting Your Footing Back

✍️ Dave Dyck, MC, CCC 📍 Nanaimo, BC 🔄 Updated July 2026

You don't know what you don't know — and after a car accident, there's a lot no one tells you. This is a growing collection of everything I wish more people understood about recovery: the anxiety, the driving fear, the concussion fog, the strain on the people you love, and what genuinely helps.

Book a Free Consultation →

Recovering from a car accident is rarely just physical. Most people I work with are also navigating anxiety that doesn't make sense to them, a brain that feels foggy or unfamiliar, and relationships that are quietly straining under the weight of it all. Change is possible, but it starts with understanding what's actually happening — which is what this guide is for.

The articles below are grouped by what you might be experiencing right now. There's no need to read them in order. Start wherever feels closest to where you are.

Support That's Often Available to You

If your accident was in British Columbia, ICBC commonly funds counselling for approved motor vehicle accident claims — including for anxiety, trauma, concussion recovery, and support for immediate family members. As an ICBC-registered provider, I can help you understand what applies to your situation.
